### Accepted requirements

- `REQ-01`–`REQ-04`: use one shared lifecycle and independently queue each
  submission without holding parent locks across provider calls.
- `REQ-05`–`REQ-08`: support synchronous and asynchronous provider completion,
  sibling aggregation, centralized job finalization, and whole-job failure when
  any submission fails.
- `REQ-09`–`REQ-13`: retry the original job without repeating completed work,
  keep provider identity on submissions, preserve ownership and idempotency
  boundaries, recover stale/lost work, and preserve graceful worker drain.

### Accepted solution

Create durable submission rows before queueing one processing task per
submission. Each task owns its provider interaction and submission state. Async
submissions use submission pollers; one job poller observes all siblings and
invokes centralized, job-type-aware finalization after all children are
terminal. Reconciliation repairs stale states and lost queue handoffs. Retrying
the original job preserves completed submissions and requeues only failed work.

### Candidate contracts to formalize

- Parent/submission identity, state, locking, queue, retry, idempotency,
  reconciliation, billing, API/admin, performance, and graceful-drain contracts.

### Required evidence

- Repository schema/lock/queue discovery and an affected-code lock audit.
- Unit, contract, race, fault, regression, smoke, and E2E evidence.
- Bounded concurrency, exactly-once finalization, reconciliation, retry, and
  graceful-drain evidence.

### Deferred work and residual risk

- Provider callbacks and proactive cancellation remain out of scope.
- A cross-feature provider-resilience policy is deferred unless repeated use
  reveals a systemic policy gap.
- Real repository facts and project approval remain required before `READY`.
